1. System Architecture & Physical Baseline

1.1 Hardware & Software Partitioning

The Chintu architecture adopts a rigorous two-tier operational hierarchy designed to decouple hard real-time dynamic balance from high-level supervision:

  • Tier 1 — Real-Time Control Core (Arduino Mega 2560):
    • 200 Hz hard real-time balance loop driven by 5 ms delta-time ticks.
    • MPU-6050 6-DOF IMU sampled via I2C Fast Mode (400 kHz) with a 2-stage complementary filter state estimator.
    • Timer3 and Timer4 CTC compare-match interrupts generating microstepped pulses for two NEMA17 stepper wheel drives up to 25 kHz.
    • Timer5 hardware/software PWM driving 6x MG995 servos for 3-link planar leg inverse kinematics.
    • Hardware E-Stop interrupt on Pin 2 (INT4) triggering sub-microsecond motor disable.
  • Tier 2 — High-Level Supervisor (Raspberry Pi 4/5):
    • FastAPI asynchronous server managing framed binary serial communication (CRC-16-CCITT) at 250,000 baud.
    • Wi-Fi Access Point host (192.168.4.1) with client isolation and nftables firewall.
    • WebSocket broadcast engine serving JSON telemetry to operators at 20 Hz.
    • Single-use ticket authentication mechanism protecting against unauthorized drive hijacking.
    • High-speed flight blackbox recorder logging telemetry to JSONL and CSV formats.
  • Operator Dashboard (Web-UI PWA):
    • Mobile/tablet optimized responsive dashboard built with Three.js (interactive 3D digital twin), real-time canvas charts, dual virtual joysticks, and safety interlocks.

1.3 Power Distribution Topology

Due to the high dynamic current draw of 6 high-torque MG995 servos (up to 15A combined stall), the power architecture enforces physical and electrical rail segregation:

  • Main Source: 3S LiPo Battery (11.1V–12.6V, 4000mAh, 30C discharge) with a 30A main blade fuse and mechanical kill switch.
  • Servo Rail (6.0V 15A BEC): Dedicated high-efficiency buck regulator with a 4700µF ultra-low-ESR capacitor buffer to absorb servo inductive kickback.
  • Computing Logic Rail (5.1V 4A BEC): Independent buck converter supplying clean USB-C power to the Raspberry Pi 4/5 and powering the Mega 2560 via USB.
  • Motor Rail (Raw 12V LiPo): Directly feeds VMOT on TMC2209/A4988 stepper drivers, decoupled with 100µF 35V low-ESR capacitors at each driver board.
  • Star Grounding: All grounds (Battery GND, BEC GND, Pi GND, Stepper GND) meet at a single central copper busbar to eliminate ground loops that cause microcontroller resets.

3. Documented Physical Breaking Points, Cracks & Singularities

The codebase and mechanical design explicitly account for several critical mechanical, electrical, and control boundaries:

3.1 Straight-Leg Kinematic Singularity ($d_{\max} = 221\text{ mm}$)

Singularity Boundary: Link lengths are Thigh $L_2 = 120\text{ mm}$ and Shank $L_3 = 101\text{ mm}$. The theoretical maximum vertical extension is $d_{\max} = L_2 + L_3 = 221\text{ mm}$.

When the knee joint approaches $0^\circ$ (straight line), the manipulator Jacobian determinant approaches zero ($\det(J) \to 0$). To accelerate the wheel hub forward or backward, the required joint angular velocity approaches infinity ($\dot{\theta} \to \infty$). Any attempt to balance in this configuration causes immediate loss of control authority. The software enforces a 46 mm safety margin, clamping maximum extension to 175 mm (180 mm in firmware).

3.2 Scissor Folding & Linkage Binding ($d_{\min} = 19\text{ mm}$)

The theoretical minimum height along the vertical axis is $|L_2 - L_3| = 19\text{ mm}$. In reality, physical MG995 servo horns, aluminum bracket flanges, and motor wiring collide well before this point. To prevent stripped servo gears and motor burnout, the system enforces a 66 mm buffer, restricting the minimum stance height to 85 mm (80 mm in firmware).

3.3 High-Current Servo Stalls & Voltage Brownout

Under aggressive maneuvering or mechanical jams, each MG995 servo can draw up to 2.5A, resulting in potential peak transient currents exceeding 15A across the 6 servos. If fed from the same rail as the microcontrollers, battery internal resistance causes bus voltage to sag below 4.5V, triggering an unexpected microcontroller brownout reset. The platform mitigates this through:

  1. Split power regulation: A dedicated 6.0V 15A buck converter with a 4700µF low-ESR capacitor buffer for servos.
  2. A debounced low-battery safety check: Battery voltage below 9.6V (3.2V/cell for 3S LiPo) must persist for $>200\text{ ms}$ before triggering a fault, preventing false trips from brief servo transients.

3.4 ATmega2560 Watchdog Bootloader Bug

The stock Arduino Mega bootloader contains a known bug: it does not clear MCUSR or disable the AVR hardware watchdog timer upon reset. When a watchdog timeout occurs, the MCU resets, but the watchdog remains active with a 15 ms timeout. The bootloader takes longer than 15 ms to run, causing the MCU to enter an infinite reset loop. Chintu protects against this by inserting an assembly hook in the .init3 section (which executes before C runtime initialization) to immediately zero MCUSR and disable the watchdog.

3.5 Critical Tilt Cutoff Boundary

An inverted pendulum has a strictly bounded basin of attraction. If chassis tilt exceeds $\pm 45^\circ$ in pitch or $\pm 35^\circ$ in roll, the wheels can no longer generate sufficient horizontal acceleration to swing the center of mass back above the contact point. Driving the motors beyond this threshold causes violent wheel spin, chassis flipping, and mechanical damage. The safety supervisor instantly halts stepper pulses and tri-states motor drivers upon crossing these thresholds.

4. Critical "Cracks" & Broken Code Points in the Codebase

Our deep audit uncovered 11 severe bugs, breaking points, and discrepancies in the repository:

Crack 1: Fatal JavaScript ReferenceError in Web UI Critical

  • File: robot/web-ui/js/api.js (Line 79)
  • Defect: 
    // api.js line 79
State.addLog('NET', `Connected to supervisor via ${protocol}//${host}:${port}`);
    The variable protocol is never declared or in scope (the script defines wsProtocol and httpProtocol).
  • Consequence: When the WebSocket connection establishes, the ws.onopen event handler throws an unhandled ReferenceError: protocol is not defined. This causes the function to abort before reaching this.startPingLoop() and this.startCommandLoop(). The UI appears connected, but keepalives and user drive commands are never sent.

Crack 2: Broken Systemd Service Module Path & Interpreter Critical

  • File: robot/backend/scripts/chintu.service (Lines 8–9)
  • Defect: 
    WorkingDirectory=/home/pi/robot/backend
ExecStart=/usr/bin/python3 -m backend.server
  • Consequence:
    1. With WorkingDirectory=/home/pi/robot/backend, Python searches for a module named backend inside backend/ and fails immediately with ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'backend'.
    2. The unit executes system Python /usr/bin/python3 instead of the virtual environment Python (/home/pi/robot/backend/venv/bin/python3), where all dependencies (FastAPI, Pydantic, Uvicorn, etc.) are installed.

Crack 3: Contradictory & Abandoned Guide in `README_RASPBERRY_PI.md` Critical

  • File: README_RASPBERRY_PI.md
  • Defect: The entire root guide instructs the user to configure an **ESP32** microcontroller at 115,200 baud running firmware/chintu_esp32.ino, or direct GPIO control via rpi_chintu.py, using scripts setup_rpi.sh and start_rpi.sh.
  • Consequence: None of these files exist in the repository. The production firmware is an Arduino Mega 2560 at 250,000 baud. The guide tells operators to execute python3 server.py --auto-serial, which crashes because server.py has no --auto-serial flag and aborts if ROBOT_AUTH_TOKEN is not defined in the environment.

Crack 4: Nominal Leg Height Discrepancy & Gain Scheduling Skew Warning

  • Files: robot/backend/config.py vs. robot/firmware/include/config.h
  • Defect:
    • Supervisor (config.py): LEG_HEIGHT_NOMINAL_MM = 135.0
    • Firmware (config.h): LEG_HEIGHT_NOMINAL_MM = 118.0f
  • Consequence: Firmware gain scheduling scales pitch gains by $\sqrt{h / h_{\text{nominal}}}$. When the supervisor commands the nominal 135 mm height, the firmware evaluates $\sqrt{135 / 118} \approx 1.07$, artificially increasing balance stiffness by 7–11% above designed baseline values. Height boundaries also conflict: supervisor restricts to [85, 175] mm, while firmware allows [80, 180] mm.

Crack 5: Missing Hardware Pin in Primary Manual Warning

  • File: robot/README.md (Section 2.1 Table)
  • Defect: The pinout table completely omits **SERVO 5 (Right Hip Pitch, Pin 12)**. It lists SERVO 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6, leaving a confusing note on SERVO 6: *(NEEDS PHYSICAL WIRING CHECK: Pin 13 is onboard LED)*.

Crack 6: Telemetry Struct Length Inconsistency Warning

  • File: robot/protocol.md (Section 2.3.2)
  • Defect: The document lists uint8_t reserved[3] for TelemetryPayload, which sums to 35 bytes, despite heading the section as Length: 36 Bytes. The actual firmware (protocol.h) and Python parser (protocol.py) implement reserved[4] (36 bytes). Any third-party client built from the markdown specification will fail CRC checks.

Crack 7: Incomplete Root `requirements.txt` Warning

  • File: requirements.txt vs. robot/backend/requirements.txt
  • Defect: The root file contains only websockets, aiohttp, and pyserial. Installing from the root omits FastAPI, Pydantic, Uvicorn, and Pytest.

Crack 8: Deprecated Hotspot Configuration in `setup_hotspot.sh` Warning

  • File: robot/backend/scripts/setup_hotspot.sh (Lines 34–38)
  • Defect: The script attempts to configure static IP by appending to /etc/dhcpcd.conf and restarting dhcpcd. Modern Raspberry Pi OS (Bookworm and later) uses NetworkManager (nmcli) and lacks dhcpcd entirely, breaking Wi-Fi setup on new systems.

Crack 9: Missing SAN in SSL Certificate Generation Warning

  • File: robot/backend/scripts/generate_cert.py (Lines 37–42)
  • Defect: The OpenSSL configuration adds SANs for 192.168.4.1, 127.0.0.1, localhost, and chintu.local. However, the system's mDNS domain throughout the repo is **chintu.robot**. Browsers connecting to https://chintu.robot:8000 flag security certificate mismatch warnings.

Crack 10: Firmware Comms Stream Parser Edge Case Info

  • File: robot/firmware/src/comms.cpp (Lines 92–98)
  • Defect: In STATE_WAIT_SYNC1, if another 0xAA byte is received (e.g. sequence 0xAA 0xAA 0x55), the state resets to STATE_WAIT_SYNC0, discarding the frame. In contrast, backend/protocol.py correctly remains in STATE_WAIT_SYNC1.

Crack 11: Architectural Gap with Research Document Critical

  • Scope: Entire codebase vs. Wheel_Leg_Robot_Research_Document.docx
  • Defect: Zero MuJoCo MJCF files (robot.xml), CAD mesh exports, PyTorch PPO scripts, or ROS 2 packages exist in the repository. The advanced simulation-to-real and RL pipelines described in the research paper remain entirely unimplemented.
